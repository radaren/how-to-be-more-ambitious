更有野心一点 How to be more ambitious

原文 / Original → Felix Rieseberg

How to be more ambitious: A tutorial for humans with models 如何更有野心：给「有模型可用」的人的一份教程

Felix Rieseberg · 13 October 2026 Felix Rieseberg · 2026 年 10 月 13 日

I work on Claude. It's an interesting experience: Every day I learn about yet another feat accomplished with or by Claude that I hadn't considered, didn't think possible, or simply lacked the curiosity and time to try. 我在做 Claude 相关的工作。这是一种很有意思的体验：每天都会听到又一件用 Claude 做成、或由 Claude 做成的事——有的我根本没想过，有的觉得不可能，有的只是缺好奇心和时间去试。

This month, it may have helped discover a new planet, I've seen the first AI music video that I really like, and we are discussing a new era of math. 这个月，它或许帮着发现了一颗新行星；我也第一次看到自己真正喜欢的 AI 音乐视频；我们还在讨论数学的新时代。

Model capabilities don't grow uniformly. Models are, as Terence Tao puts it, "astounding in some directions, while inadequate in others". 模型能力并不是均匀增长的。用陶哲轩（Terence Tao）的话说，模型「在某些方向上令人惊叹，在另一些方向上却不够用」。

Some people manage to pilot the astounding capabilities and seem to be flying - it's addicting to watch people reach incredible heights with the products I've built. 有些人能驾驭那些惊人的能力，看起来像在飞——看着别人用我参与打造的产品冲到难以置信的高度，会上瘾。

Many others are still struggling to get their new wings to work, wondering what they're good for beyond asking "What is the capital of France" or "Can you make this email sound less angry". 还有很多人仍在努力让新翅膀飞起来，琢磨除了问「法国的首都是哪儿」或「能不能把这封邮件改得没那么冲」之外，它们到底还能干什么。

I frequently proclaim (together with other people working on AI) that we ought to be more ambitious when using it. It's become a rallying cry. 我和许多做 AI 的人经常宣称：用模型时，我们应该更有野心。这几乎成了一句口号。

Whenever we see something impressive, we repost and write "we're not ambitious enough". I understand how tricky it is to follow that advice, so here's a tutorial on being more ambitious. 一看到令人印象深刻的事，就转发并写下「我们还不够有野心」。我知道这条建议其实很难照做，所以下面是一份「如何更有野心」的教程。

I think of ambition in four types: Speed, Altitude, Depth, and Range. 我把野心分成四种：速度（Speed）、高度（Altitude）、深度（Depth）、广度（Range）。

  • Speed: The same work in less time, the bread and butter for AI's economic value: If you need some code written, I'll do it faster with Claude. Well established and requires little creativity in how you approach AI, so I'll skip this one. 速度：用更少时间做同样的事——AI 经济价值里最扎实的那一块：你需要写点代码，用 Claude 会更快。这条路已经很成熟，对「怎么用 AI」也几乎不要求创造力，所以这篇跳过它。
  • Altitude: Going up in abstraction and considering the outcome rather than the individual task. 高度：抬高抽象层级，关注最终结果，而不是单个任务本身。
  • Depth: Considering a task the way a field-leading expert would. Rather than operating at the task's layer, we go one level deeper and touch the underlying data, tools, and problems. 深度：像领域顶尖专家那样看待任务。不停留在任务表层，再往下一层，碰到底层数据、工具和真正的问题。
  • Range: Drawing on multiple fields rather than just the one the task might need at the surface, using knowledge, tools, and techniques you normally wouldn't. 广度：调动多个领域，而不只是任务表面上似乎需要的那一个；用上你平时不会用的知识、工具和技法。

I primarily use Claude (duh) but my advice is not model-specific. 我主要用 Claude（废话），但我的建议并不绑定某个模型。

Altitude 高度

We often flippantly call this "letting the model operate at a higher abstraction". When you give the model a task, it's executing your best guess at how to get the outcome you want. 我们常随口说这叫「让模型在更高抽象层上工作」。你把任务交给模型时，它其实是在执行你对「如何得到想要结果」的最佳猜测。

To be more ambitious in altitude, ask yourself - what do you want to happen next? What's the goal beyond the goal? 想在「高度」上更有野心，先问自己：接下来你真正希望发生什么？目标背后的目标是什么？

At the beginning of last year, I still asked Claude to fix individual crashes for me. Eventually I asked Claude to simply keep our apps crash-free. 去年年初，我还在让 Claude 帮我修一个个崩溃。后来我改成只要求一件事：让我们的应用保持不崩溃。

Let's consider an everyday (dare I say mundane?) office task. You're at Acme Inc, an imagined company, where you need to put together a deck for the quarterly business review (QBR). 来看一个日常（敢说有点无聊吗？）的办公任务。你在虚构公司 Acme Inc，要为一场季度业务复盘（QBR）做一份 deck。

Given a bunch of data, Claude will happily make you some slides. Maybe you even have a template and it just needs to fill it in. 给它一堆数据，Claude 会很乐意帮你做几页幻灯片。也许你还有模板，它只要往里填。

That's hardly comparable to finding a new planet, so let's look at some concrete steps to make our approach more ambitious. We'll start basic and get a little bit more ambitious with each step: 这当然没法跟发现新行星比，所以我们看几步具体做法，让整条路径更有野心。从基础开始，每一步再抬高一点：

  • As input, most reviews want to know what changed this quarter, why, and what should be done about it. Resist the urge to just update whatever numbers are in the template - give the model too as much data as you can get your hands on so it can understand your business: "Tell me what changed this quarter, why, and what we should do about it." 作为输入，多数复盘想知道：这季度变了什么、为什么、该怎么办。别急着只改模板里的数字——把你能拿到的数据尽量都给模型，让它理解你的业务：「告诉我这季度变了什么、为什么，以及我们该怎么办。」
  • Meetings (the good kind, anyway) ideally end with some decisions. Ask Claude: "Work out which three decisions are most urgent in the face of everything you've learned, and start working on potential directions with for/against arguments." 会议（好的那种）最好以若干决策收场。问 Claude：「基于你了解到的一切，算出哪三个决策最紧迫，并开始梳理可能方向，附上正反论据。」
  • QBRs are from a time when good reviews required humans to carefully sift through data, figure out what the data means, and if the business should adjust. Ask Claude: "The review exists so leadership catches problems early and moves money and people to the right places. Design a better way to do that than a quarterly deck, show from our own history that it would have caught things sooner, and run it beside the current one for a quarter." QBR 诞生于「好的复盘需要人仔细筛数据、弄清含义、判断业务要不要调整」的年代。问 Claude：「复盘存在的意义，是让管理层尽早发现问题，并把钱和人挪到对的地方。请设计一种比季度 deck 更好的做法；用我们自己的历史证明它能更早发现问题；并和现行方式并行跑一个季度。」

The altitude approach works best if you really are after some ultimate outcome; it works less well for creative endeavors. 「高度」这条路，最适合你真的在追逐某个终极结果时；对创造性工作则没那么灵。

A good tell-tale sign that ambition in altitude is a good idea is if you're going to have meetings to discuss whatever you're working on. 一个很好的信号是：如果你接下来要开会讨论手头这件事——那在高度上提高野心往往就对了。

There are currently limits ("Claude, go make money" doesn't seem to work for most businesses by itself in 2026), but I generally see that models seem to be able to operate at higher levels of abstraction the more capable they become. 目前仍有边界（2026 年，「Claude，去赚钱」对大多数公司单凭这一句似乎还不管用），但我总体看到：模型越强，就越能在更高抽象层上运转。

Altitude is also where people find durable workflows that take the human out of the loop, making it less necessary for you to constantly update the model with task after task. 高度也是人们找到「可持久工作流」、把人从回路里拿出来的地方——你不必再不停地一件件派任务给模型。

Doing this well often involves changing your workflow - in order to let Claude make sure our app is crash-free, I first had to make sure it can easily fetch crash reports, understand which app version maps to what git commit, and give it access to Windows, macOS, and Linux computers so it can actually independently reproduce and verify fixes. 要把这件事做好，常常要改你自己的工作流——为了让 Claude 保证应用不崩溃，我得先让它方便拉崩溃报告，搞清哪个应用版本对应哪个 git commit，并给它 Windows、macOS、Linux 机器的访问权，好让它能独立复现并验证修复。

Depth 深度

Sometimes, you just want a task executed really well. Being ambitious in depth means pushing the model to use the tools, techniques, media, and knowledge employed by the best professionals in the field - and to keep pushing until even those people might learn something new from your work. 有时，你只是想把一件事做得特别好。在深度上更有野心，意味着推动模型用上该领域顶尖专业人士所用的工具、技法、媒介和知识——并一直推到连那些人也可能从你的结果里学到新东西。

Let's continue with our Acme Inc example of a quarterly business review deck. Assume for a second that Acme Inc struggles with some user churn (customer loss over time). I've found a mix of the following techniques useful: 继续用 Acme Inc 的季度业务复盘 deck 举例。假设 Acme Inc 正为用户流失（随时间失去客户）头疼。我发现下面这些技法混着用很有用：

  • Insist on the same instruments used by the best in the industry: When prompted to act like a professional, models often switch vocabulary without actually switching methods. To work on our problem with depth, we need to use the same tools as the best data scientists and statisticians. When Claude reaches for markdown tables, insist on the use of databases and statistical models. You don't necessarily have to know what the experts use, Claude usually does. 坚持用行业顶尖那一套工具：一被要求「像专业人士」，模型常常只换了词汇，方法没换。要在深度上推进，就要用顶尖数据科学家和统计学家同款工具。Claude 伸手去拿 markdown 表格时，坚持要数据库和统计模型。你未必知道专家用什么，Claude 通常知道。
  • Pick unreasonable goals and tell the model that they're very reasonable: You need your website to load in 5s instead of 10s? Ask for 200ms. Does something seem impossible to the model? Reiterate that it's very much solvable, it just needs to keep going. 定下不合理的目标，并告诉模型它们其实很合理：网站要从 10 秒加载压到 5 秒？直接要 200 毫秒。模型觉得不可能？反复强调：完全可解，只要继续做下去。
  • Challenge what you know: Especially in business, a lot of things we believe to be true are rough approximations of reality. Give the model access to the raw information and data and ask it to adversarially check assumptions. "We believe churn is an onboarding problem. Try to reproduce that from the raw data." 挑战你以为知道的事：尤其在商业里，很多「我们相信为真」的东西只是现实的粗糙近似。把原始信息和数据交给模型，让它对抗式地检验假设。「我们相信流失是 onboarding 问题。请试着用原始数据复现这一点。」
  • Produce new knowledge: Easily tacked onto challenging what you know - once Claude is already deep inside the data, ask it to find blind spots. "Build a simulation of our customers that reproduces the last year from our logs. Then find the earliest signal of churn we don't know about yet, and show it predicts on data it has not seen." 产出新知识：很容易接在「挑战已知」后面——Claude 已经深潜在数据里时，让它找盲点。「用日志建一个能复现过去一年的客户仿真。然后找出我们尚未知晓的、最早的流失信号，并证明它能在未见数据上预测。」
  • Use an outside judge: Do not ask the model to judge itself. I assume that this will eventually be solved with increasing model intelligence, but in October 2026, I implore you to insist on outside validation. Ideally that's a tool the model can run to get feedback immediately. 用外部裁判：别让模型自评。我猜随着模型变强这终会被解决，但在 2026 年 10 月，我恳请你坚持外部验证。最好是一个模型能立刻跑起来拿反馈的工具。

A simple prompt that often helps me: 一个常常帮到我的简单提示词：

I want a deeper version of this task, not a bigger one. Before rewriting it: (1) say what a top professional in this field would do here that I'm not asking for; (2) name the rawest material they would start from instead of what has already been digested for me; (3) name the specific methods and tools they would use; (4) say what, other than you or me, would show the result is right. Then rewrite my prompt to ask for exactly that. 我要的是这个任务的「更深」版本，不是更大的版本。先别改写，请先：（1）说出该领域顶尖专业人士会做、而我还没要求的事；（2）点名他们会从何种「最原始」材料起步，而不是我已经被人消化过的材料；（3）点名他们会用的具体方法和工具；（4）说出除了你或我之外，还有什么能证明结果是对的。然后再改写我的提示词，去精确要求这些。

Range 广度

In short, being ambitious in range means that we consider our work from the perspective of different fields - more fields than would have been economical in the past. 简言之，在广度上更有野心，意味着从不同领域的视角看待工作——比过去「划算」时能用到的领域还要多。

This isn't a particularly unique insight, every single heist movie spends the first 30 minutes assembling a diverse cast of characters and talents. 这并不特别新鲜：每部劫案片前 30 分钟，都在凑齐一班各怀绝技的角色。

As AI products make it easier and easier to run multi-agent systems (with multiple threads, orchestrators, or simply subagents), I've seen really interesting results by imploring Claude to bring in additional fields. 随着 AI 产品让多 agent 系统（多线程、编排器，或干脆子 agent）越来越好跑，我发现恳请 Claude 引入额外领域时，会出现非常有意思的结果。

Putting this into practice isn't easy; it requires you to be creative. Until I saw my colleague Alex Albert use Blender to render interior designs, I never considered using Claude for 3D environments for my homepage - even though I've previously made video games! 落地并不容易，需要你有创造力。直到看见同事 Alex Albert 用 Blender 渲染室内设计，我才想到可以用 Claude 给主页做 3D 环境——尽管我以前做过游戏！

However, this is easily where I see the most magic happen. 然而，这也是我最常看到「魔法」发生的地方。

So, how do we do this? I use the following techniques: 那怎么做？我用下面这些技法：

  • Try to replace the nouns with verbs: An old trick from The Art of Innovation by Tom Kelley and Jonathan Littman. A quarterly review isn't "a deck". It's "getting busy people to believe a story about cause and effect, and to decide under uncertainty, in 30 minutes". Having the verbs makes it easier for both me and Claude to understand which fields might bring in a unique and useful perspective. 试着用动词替换名词：这是 Tom Kelley 与 Jonathan Littman《创新的艺术》（The Art of Innovation）里的老把戏。季度复盘不是「一份 deck」，而是「在 30 分钟内，让忙碌的人相信一个因果故事，并在不确定下做决定」。有了动词，我和 Claude 都更容易看出哪些领域能带来独特而有用的视角。
  • Assemble the crew: The deck needs to be amazing, otherwise aliens will blow up Earth. What kind of team will humanity get together? For production quality, I'd bring in an analyst, a journalist, and an editor. If it has to be presented, probably a motion and audio designer, too. It might be useful to employ an adversarial "red team", too, so let's bring in a short seller and a forensic accountant. It is easy to accidentally engage in role play, which will make your transcript weirder without improving the actual outcome. Use this method to find fields and techniques. Don't ask the model to write like a short seller, ask it to do what a short seller does to research their position. 组建船员：这份 deck 必须惊艳，否则外星人会炸掉地球。人类会凑怎样一支队伍？要生产质量，我会拉来分析师、记者和编辑；若还要上台讲，大概还要动态与音频设计师。对抗性「红队」也可能有用，那就再请来做空研究员和法务会计师。很容易不小心滑进角色扮演——对话记录会更怪，结果未必更好。用这方法是为了找领域和方法。别让模型「像做空者那样写」，而要它去做空者研究仓位时真正会做的事。

All the ways in which I describe ambition require the model to have a computer with the ability to install software and access to all kinds of relevant data (either using MCP connectors or other means). Plenty of tools make this possible and I recommend that you use one. 我描述的每一种野心，都需要模型拥有一台能装软件的计算机，并能接触各类相关数据（用 MCP 连接器或其他方式）。有很多工具能做到这一点，我建议你用上其中一个。

We have been here before 我们曾经来过这里

That's my concrete advice for how to be more ambitious. If you're willing to stick around for a minute, I'd like to tell you why I think this is even necessary in the first place. 以上就是我关于「如何更有野心」的具体建议。若你愿意再多待一分钟，我想说说为什么我觉得这件事从一开始就有必要。

We humans have always been held back by what we believe possible - and what's worse, we usually trust ourselves only in very narrow disciplines. 我们人类一直被「自己相信什么是可能的」所限制——更糟的是，我们通常只在极窄的学科里信任自己。

Michelangelo famously didn't want to paint the Sistine Chapel - you see, he was a sculptor, not a painter. 米开朗基罗出名地不想画西斯廷礼拜堂——你看，他是雕塑家，不是画家。

While researching the problem of ambition, I learned that psychology has a name for this: self-efficacy. In short, people with high self-efficacy believe in their own abilities more, and in turn actually accomplish more. 研究「野心」这个问题时，我得知心理学给它起了个名字：自我效能（self-efficacy）。简言之，自我效能高的人更相信自己的能力，结果也真的做成更多事。

There's evidence that self-efficacy can be hacked: Locke and Latham pored over 35 years of goal-setting research to confidently proclaim that humans who are given specific hard goals perform better than those who are simply told to try their best. 有证据表明自我效能可以被「黑」一下：Locke 与 Latham 啃了 35 年目标设定研究后自信宣称——被给予具体而艰难目标的人，表现会好过只被要求「尽力而为」的人。

Stripe founder Patrick Collison maintains a "Fast" list, chronicling the many things humans have accomplished when given both audacious goals and tight deadlines. Stripe 创始人 Patrick Collison 维护着一份「Fast」清单，记录人类在同时拥有大胆目标与紧迫期限时做成的许多事。

Some hacks that are well documented: 一些有据可查的「黑客手法」：

  • Accomplish hard things by believing them to be possible: In 1939, George Dantzig showed up late to a statistics class at Berkeley, copied two problems off the blackboard, and handed in his solutions thinking they were homework. They were two unsolved problems in statistics. 靠相信「可能」做成难事：1939 年，George Dantzig 迟到进了伯克利一堂统计课，从黑板上抄下两道题当作业交了上去。那其实是两道未解的统计难题。
  • Accomplish hard things after watching somebody else do them: For decades, humans chased the four-minute mile. Roger Bannister broke it in May 1954 but remained the only human to do so for a mere 46 days. If you're into running, we're watching something similar happen to the two-hour marathon: Sabastian Sawe ran the first official sub-two-hour marathon in London this April. In the same race, the runner-up also broke the two-hour mark. 看别人做成后再做成难事：几十年来人类追逐「四分钟一英里」。Roger Bannister 在 1954 年 5 月打破纪录，却仅独享了 46 天。如果你爱跑步，我们正看着类似的事发生在两小时马拉松上：今年 4 月 Sabastian Sawe 在伦敦跑出首个官方破两小时；同场亚军也破了两小时。
  • Accomplish hard things by giving yourself permission to fail: Apparently everyone's favorite business book is The Inner Game of Tennis, which I discovered just last week. The book's core thesis is that you're not playing your best game of tennis if you're trying really, really hard. We engineers might remember Linux, announced as "just a hobby, won't be big and professional like gnu". 给自己失败许可，从而做成难事：似乎人人最爱的商业书是《网球的内心游戏》（The Inner Game of Tennis）——我上周才发现。核心论点是：你若拼了命地用力，反而打不出最好的网球。工程师或许记得 Linux 的宣布：「只是个爱好，不会像 gnu 那样又大又专业。」

Self-efficacy isn't a new problem, but it is a more powerful one now. In a world where our abilities make leaps every couple of weeks, we need techniques that allow us to do things we didn't really consider or believe possible. 自我效能不是新问题，但现在它更有杀伤力。在能力每隔几周就跃迁的世界里，我们需要一些技法，去做那些我们原本没真正想过、或不相信可能的事。

If you find more techniques that allow you to be more ambitious, please let me know! 如果你找到更多能让自己更有野心的技法，请告诉我！
